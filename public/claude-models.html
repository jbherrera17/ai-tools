Which Claude model, and when

Four models, four jobs. Route each task to the cheapest model that clears your quality bar — not to the most capable one by reflex.

The decision tree

Work top to bottom. Stop at the first question that fits the task in front of you.

1. Does the task run for hours without a human in the loop?

Yes, and being wrong is expensive → Fable 5
Yes, and it is mostly coding → Opus 5

2. Is it complex judgment, architecture, or hard engineering?

Yes — accuracy outweighs cost → Opus 5
It also needs current knowledge → Opus 5 (freshest cutoff)

3. Will this run repeatedly in production?

Yes — normal quality bar → Sonnet 5
Yes, and it is simple and high-volume → Haiku 4.5

4. Does a person wait on the response?

Yes, and speed is the product → Haiku 4.5
Yes, but answer quality matters more → Sonnet 5

5. Are the inputs long — big codebases, many documents?

Over 200k tokens → Fable 5, Opus 5, or Sonnet 5
Comfortably under 200k → Haiku 4.5 is in play

6. Still unsure?

Start here, then tune → Opus 5

Two ways to start

Efficiency-first

Build on Haiku 4.5, test the use case hard, and upgrade only where you hit a real capability gap. Cheap to iterate, and it forces the expensive model to earn its place.

Tradeoff: you can mistake a prompt problem for a model problem, then engineer around a limit that was never there.

Capability-first

Build on Opus 5, tune the prompts, then step down to cheaper models as the workflow stabilizes. You see the real quality ceiling on day one.

Tradeoff: the step-down often never happens, and unit economics quietly bloat.

The lever most people miss

Recent Opus and Sonnet models take an effort parameter that trades intelligence against latency and cost inside a single model. Tuning effort is often better than switching models. On Opus 5, start at the default and step up for the most demanding agentic work.

Then settle it with evidence: benchmark your own prompts and data before you commit a production budget.

Notes

  • Sonnet 5 carries introductory pricing of $2 / $10 per million tokens through August 31, 2026. The $3 / $15 rate takes effect September 1, 2026.
  • Claude Mythos 5 shares Fable 5's specs and pricing but is not generally available — access runs through Anthropic's invitation-only Project Glasswing.
  • Fable 5 routes a small share of queries to Opus 5 under its safeguards layer. Anthropic reports this triggers in under 5% of sessions.
  • Haiku 4.5 supports extended thinking; Fable 5, Opus 5, and Sonnet 5 use adaptive thinking, always on for Fable 5.
  • Every model ID is a pinned snapshot, not an evergreen pointer. Pin deliberately.
  • Specs verified against Anthropic's model documentation on July 25, 2026. Pricing and availability change — confirm before committing a budget.
